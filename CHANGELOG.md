# Changelog

All notable changes to Manuscript (formerly Textbox Tools) are listed here, newest first.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Cheat sheet**: Getting started has "Put cheat sheet on this page": a picture "Manuscript · editing marks" with eight examples whose text says what the mark does (delete, replace, insert, one letter, exact letters, space, close up, swap), "+ text" where the mark takes new words, goes onto the current page, to move with the lasso and erase like any picture. Manuscript never reads it as a mark. Where the picture can't be inserted, it is saved to EXPORT as manuscript-cheatsheet.png instead.
- **Getting started**: the help (?) in Manuscript starts with what Manuscript does, the toolbar buttons, the core marks and the first things to do; it opens by itself once, the first time Manuscript opens. Its last line shows the plugin version.
- **Report a problem** (Manuscript → Settings → Diagnostics): one file in EXPORT for a bug report, with the version, device, settings, how the last marks were read, the timings and the errors of this session. Your text is only in it when you tick "Include the text of this page". It replaces Save Diagnostics and Save timings.
- Settings → Diagnostics shows the plugin version and the device in its first line.
- When a screen fails, it shows "Something went wrong on this screen" with Report a problem and Close instead of an empty card.
- Comments and images: the new lasso button "Comment or Image" keeps handwriting beside the text as a comment or an image. A small card asks Comment · Image (or Cancel); a comment gets a speech bubble with "C" on the page, an image a plain double frame. They have no number and never change the text: Apply leaves them and their signs alone.
  - Text tagged with "Insertion or Replacement" that has no mark can become a comment or an image, and back: on the "No mark found" card (Select mark now · Comment · Image · Later), on "Not a clear mark" for the text that waits for its mark (the strokes you just selected stay plain handwriting), and in the review under To decide. Its "?" becomes the bubble or the frame.
  - Each comment and image belongs to the paragraph beside it: the one it is written next to, else the one above it, else the one below, on its own page, in the text or the "Set aside" area, and within its own linked file. Headings count.
  - Manuscript has a fourth tab, Notes, after New text · Marks · To decide: the comments and images of the pages read, with the paragraph each belongs to ("Comment · ¶ 1", "Image · after ¶ 2"); the page strip counts them ("p.3 · 2 notes"). A comment: Read (it is recognized only when you ask), Correct, choose another paragraph with ‹ Paragraph above · Paragraph below › (also on the page before or after), Done ✓ (it stays, greyed), Use as text › (back to tagged text with a "?"), Drop (the sign goes, the handwriting stays). An image: the same ‹ ›, Place Before or After its paragraph, Update image (its picture from the handwriting as it is now) and Drop. Images are written to the linked file in a later version; comments are not exported.
  - A comment or image on a page without any paragraph is listed under To decide, with ‹ Paragraph above · Paragraph below › and Drop.
  - The Outline shows how many comments ("C 2") and images ("▣ 1") belong to each paragraph, for the whole note; moving or setting aside such a paragraph says "Its comments stay where they are (moving them comes later)."
  - Help ("?" in Manuscript) has a page "Comments and images".

- Fine marks: change single letters, spaces and punctuation by pen.
  - A strike with a short line across each end deletes exactly what is between them, from one letter to several words, even inside a word ("redd|ish|" → "red", "kind|ness|" → "kind"); for one letter the strike between the lines may be very short. With a small circle too (after the strike, above it or at the right line's top), the tagged text replaces exactly that part ("simp|licity|" + "le" → "simple"); the number goes into the circle. Lines inside a strike, away from its ends, mark nothing. A strike alone or with a small circle still takes whole words.
  - A slash through one letter deletes it, or, with tagged text after it, replaces it ("word" + slash on d + "ds" → "words"). A slash may slant down to 30°; one wider than two letters is marked "check the letter".
  - A small circle drawn above a slash or between a caret's legs holds the number Textbox Tools puts there.
  - Close up: a flat arc under (or over) the gap between two words removes the space; a dash or comma at the gap goes too when you tick "Also remove". At a line end, draw the arc twice — after the last word and before the first word of the next line; drawn after a paragraph's last word and before the first word of the paragraph right below it, the two arcs join the paragraphs.
  - Punctuation: a ∧ or V with a small circle between its legs and the mark written inside (, ; . : ' -) puts the mark in at the caret's tip, with no tagging. Textbox Tools reads the mark itself; anything it can't read waits for one tap on the right mark in the review (also `?`), so a letter never goes in by mistake. Commas and full stops attach to the word before; a hyphen in a gap joins the two words.
  - Spaces: a caret in a gap adds words with spaces, between letters it adds letters without; deleting or replacing exactly never leaves two words glued together.
  - Tagged text recognized as one or two characters gets quick buttons for punctuation in the review.
- Insert right here: tagged text without a usable mark can go in as a new paragraph where its handwriting starts — between the paragraphs it is written between, or as a new textbox where it is when no paragraph is near. Offered in the review under To decide.

- A place in the note for material that isn't part of the text: Set aside in the Outline moves a paragraph or a section to pages at the end of the note, under one heading "Set aside" (with a NOTE title, so it is in the table of contents). Everything below that heading stays in the note only: it is never written to a linked file or exported to DOCX, also what you type there or move there with the lasso. Sort it under your own headings typed on those pages. Show › Set aside in the Outline lists only that area, with its headings, and explains it while it is empty; the other Show choices list only the text. Move back puts a paragraph from there into the text, at the heading or file you choose, and then shows it there. Text that grows in an Outline apply gets new pages before the area, so it never runs into it, and a file added to a linked note goes before it too. Notes with blocks set aside before get their "Set aside" heading on a page of its own at the next Outline apply.

- Manuscript has a third tab, Files: the note's linked files (as before under Details), and what goes into the note and out of it — Import a text file ›, Import a whole folder › (on a note with linked files: Add a file ›) and Export DOCX ›, which open inside the card; their Layout › and Classification rules › lines lead to Settings. After an import the card reads the note again and shows the import's result on Files. A note without textboxes opens on Files.
- Clean up layout (Manuscript → Settings → Layout): lays the whole note, or one linked file, out again with the current margins and sizes — every textbox full width in the size of its level, the usual gap between them, files and the chosen headings on a new page. Paragraphs too long for a page are cut to fit, and pieces cut before become one textbox again when they fit. Handwriting stays where it is (the check names its pages); pages left without text are removed, and for one file pages are added or removed before the next file, so the files after it don't move. It is planned in the Outline ("Clean up layout: whole note") together with other changes and applied with "Apply clean up", after the edits on its pages; one backup first.
- Manuscript Outline in three panes: on the left the Contents — linked files and headings by level, the open one filled black, ✎ with the edits waiting in its own text; on the right the open entry's own paragraphs (up to its first subheading) with what follows below them; underneath, the panel for what you tapped last. Show All · With edits · Not final greys out headings without matches and folds their subheadings in. A heading's Level (¶ · # · ## · ###) changes alone or with its subheadings (both shown before you choose); a paragraph becomes a heading the same way. Paragraphs move ↑ Up, ↓ Down, ⤒ Top, ⤓ End within their heading, or Move to… another heading (also in another file): the Contents show → where it can go. Files move ↑ ↓ or Move before… another file. A heading's section can be set aside or deleted (⋯) as a whole. Everything is planned first and applied together. The controls are text now, not framed buttons; a note without headings shows one list. Settings → Outline sets the level of a heading under your own title, which levels get a title, Restore titles and Read the outline again.
- Headings in the table of contents: after an import or an Outline apply, every `#` and `##` heading gets a NOTE title (black for #, dark grey for ##), so it shows in the note's table of contents; a heading whose level changes gets the title of its new level, and one that becomes a paragraph loses ours. Titles you made yourself are never touched, and a textbox under one counts as a heading. Restore titles (Settings → Outline) puts them right on every page.
- Import: Choose › opens the file selector in the folder the current note sits in.
- Bind a whole folder to a note: Import → "Whole folder" imports the chosen file's folder, each file under its name as a title. "Files and order" chooses the files (one left out isn't offered again) and whether the folder is ordered: an ordered folder keeps the note's order of the files in its index.txt (index.md in Obsidian, a plain list of [[links]] written by the plugin); a folder that has an index.txt is ordered by it. Manuscript names the folder in its header and says when it has new files; the Files tab adds them after the last page (or leaves them out), switches between ordered and unordered, and offers "Rewrite index.txt" when index.txt was changed in Obsidian (it is never overwritten otherwise). Update writes index.txt after the files when the order changed.
- Text moved from one linked file to another (a paragraph moved under another file's title, or a file's title deleted on the page) is never written silently: Update asks "Move it to … in the files too?" — Move it writes it where it is now (and doesn't ask again for it), Keep apart leaves it in its own file this time.
- Outline: a linked file's title shows with § and moves with its whole file, its headings included; a heading's section ends at the next file. A file's title is never set aside or deleted on its own.
- Several linked files in one note: on a note with linked files, Import becomes "Add a file to this note" and puts the file after the last page, linked. Every linked file starts with its name as a NOTE title, so it shows in the note's table of contents, and everything below it up to the next file's title belongs to it (typed or split textboxes too). Manuscript names the file or "3 files" in its header; Apply also updates every file that changed ("Also update changed files"), and "Update 2 files" writes them without marks. A file changed in Obsidian since the last write, or no longer found, is not written and named; the others are. The Files tab lists the files with their state (in sync, changed here, changed in Obsidian, …), and for the chosen file Save as copy, Save to EXPORT, Check write access and Unlink; Restore titles puts missing titles back. The Outline moves blocks on pages with these titles and the titles follow. A note linked before keeps working; it gets no title.
- Write a linked text file back: Manuscript names the file of a linked import in its header. "Apply n edits" also updates the file ("Also update <file>", ticked every time the card opens); with no marks waiting but the note changed (typed in a textbox, split, joined, moved in the Outline), the button is "Update <file> · n changed blocks". Untouched text is written back exactly as it was, edited headings keep their #s, new textboxes are written as typed, framed "kept as is" blocks are written where their placeholder sits, and blocks set aside in the Outline are left out. Before a "kept as is" block whose placeholder you deleted leaves the file, the card asks (Remove / Keep it). A file changed outside since the last write is never overwritten; the Files tab offers Save as copy, Save to EXPORT, and lists warnings (a link that may be broken, a list whose indentation changed, missing blocks). The original text is kept once in the plugin folder before the first write.
- Import a text file linked to its file: in the Paragraph boxes layout, "Link to file (write changes back)" (on by default) places one textbox per heading, paragraph or list of the file and remembers where each came from, so the changes can be written back to the file later. Frontmatter stays out of the note and is kept. Code blocks, formulas, tables, quotes and callouts, HTML, comments, embeds and horizontal lines are kept exactly as they are: the note shows a framed placeholder for each ("⟦Table · 5 rows · kept as is⟧") that can be moved but isn't edited. The preview says what the file holds that is kept as is. Manuscript → Files shows the linked file, with "Check write access" and "Unlink".
- Manuscript opens faster the second time: pages read before show at once, with their changes. The current page is checked first, the others quietly after it; a page that changed is read again and its list updated ("Page 5 changed since it was read"). Tapping a page checks it again. Apply, Drop and the Outline still read their pages fresh before they write. The Outline shows the last scan at once and checks it in the background.
- Manuscript Settings → Diagnostics → Speed: the plugin records how long reading pages, recognizing and applying take. "Save timings" writes them as one file to the EXPORT folder (no text in it), to hand over when something feels slow; "Clear" starts over.
- Manuscript: "Go to page N ›" closes the card and shows that page in NOTE — in the Review for the chosen page (or the selected change's page), in the Outline for the selected block.
- Manuscript Outline: move a block by tapping ← at the place it should go (between two blocks, or at the end), set it aside to a new page at the end of the note, or delete it. Changes are planned first and shown in place (moved underlined, set aside in italics, deleted struck through); Undo per block or Undo last. "Apply n changes" lays out the pages from the first change on, keeping the usual spacing, with one backup. Edits come first: a block with pending edits can't be moved, and pages with handwriting outside textboxes or not yet read are named instead of being rebuilt.
- Manuscript covers the whole note: a strip with every page and its count of changes (✓ none, ? not read yet); tap a page to see only its changes, or "Read n pages" to read the rest. Apply applies the ready changes of every page read, with one backup and one save.
- Manuscript Outline: a second tab lists every textbox of the note as a block in reading order (H for a heading, ¶ for a paragraph), with its first and last line, ✎ and the number of edits still pending on it, and its status on the right (○ open, ◐ review, ● final). Filter by pending edits or by status. Tap a block to read it whole in the panel below and set its status; "Review its edits" jumps to its page in the Review. The status is kept in the plugin (block-status.json); a textbox gets a small tag the first time its status is set.
- Manuscript: the NOTE toolbar button "Apply All Edit Markers" is now "Manuscript", a new card for the page's edit marks. The list shows every change in reading order with its changed text in full (long insertions and swaps too) and a few words around it; the selected change (the first one when the card opens) shows on the right with more of its text, the recognized text of tagged handwriting to correct (high up, so the writing pad doesn't cover it), its edges and Drop. Drop asks on the right, so the list never moves. Marks and tagged text that couldn't be used are listed under To decide.
- A swap's edges can be fine-tuned too: the start of the first part, where the parts meet, and the end of the second part, each by one character.
- The edit-marker help ("?" in the review) now shows the marks: first all marks at a glance, each with a small picture of its shape, what it looks like and whether it needs tagged text (tap a row to go to it); then every mark with a picture of a marked textbox and the text after Apply, how to draw it and what to keep in mind; then a short guide to the review. Numbers that Textbox Tools puts on the page are shown in grey, so it's clear you don't write them.
- A new lasso toolbar button, "Insertion or Replacement": lasso handwritten new text and tap it to tag the text, without converting it to text first. The mark drawn just before the text decides what it is: a replace mark makes it a replacement, a caret an insertion. Only a mark drawn right before the text counts; strokes that already belong to tagged text (its handwriting or its mark) are never taken. Without a mark, the card opens at once and is ready as soon as the text is tagged. The tap is quick and opens no screen: a circled number appears left of the text, and the same number at its mark. A replace mark can now also be the delete line plus a small circle drawn right after its end; the number goes into that circle. The tagged handwriting and mark are found again even after you move them with the lasso.
- The edit-marker review recognizes tagged text while you read, one at a time, the selected change first; until then a grey placeholder stands in the text. A picture of the handwriting shows below its text; tap the text to fix it right there with the Supernote keyboard's handwriting input. Skip for later and Recognize again are next to it. Apply changes everything that is ready, puts insertions in as new paragraphs, and removes the used marks and tagged handwriting; the rest stays for a later pass. Recognized text and your choices are kept, so the review can be closed and opened again.
- The circle of a replace mark may also sit just above the line, anywhere along the struck words.
- The picture of tagged handwriting is the one taken when tagging; if none was kept, the handwriting is drawn from its stored strokes.
- Tagging is faster: the circles and numbers are prepared at once, the search for the replace mark stops as soon as it has found it, and the plugin's own files are read and written while the Supernote works on the page instead of afterwards. A short pause after the lasso closes keeps the Supernote's note app from crashing when the tag follows too quickly.
- Words are now placed where the Supernote actually draws them (about 15 px right of the textbox edge), so carets and delete markers pick the word you meant, also short words at a line's start or end.
- Paragraph marks: a < or > between two lines, or a step mark (under the line, straight up, on above the next word) at a word gap, breaks the paragraph there; followed by tagged text, the text becomes a paragraph of its own there. Each part becomes its own textbox; paragraphs below move down. V and ∧ insert words.
- Insertions go where a caret points: draw a V or ∧ on the line, or a < or > beside it (one stroke or two lines meeting at the tip), write the new text right after, lasso it and tap Insertion or Replacement. The text goes in between words (spaces added), inside a word, or as a new paragraph when the caret is in the space between two paragraphs. The caret's number sits between the ends of its legs. Paragraphs that would be covered by longer text or a new paragraph move down.
- Select mark: when no mark is found just before tagged text, the card offers "Select mark now" — draw the mark (or lasso the one you drew) and tap Insertion or Replacement again within 5 minutes; the text never needs to be rewritten. The review's To decide offers Select mark too, and the review opens again at that change once the mark is found. A mark selected this way may be a delete line alone.
- Drop: a change you don't want, or an unused mark or tagged text under To decide, can be taken off the page without applying it. Drop asks first; for tagged text you choose to keep your handwriting (only the numbers and the mark go) or to erase it too (a backup is written first). Strokes away from any textbox are never offered, so your own handwriting stays.
- Fine-tune every change by one character: below the selected change, Start and End (or Place for an insertion or paragraph break) show the characters around its edges; ‹ › move them, and a line shows how the text will read. A comma right after a crossed-out word now stays unless the line reaches it.
- "Check place" marks an insertion whose caret's tip was between two lines or inside a word, with the reason.

### Changed

- The help says plainly when tagged words belong to a mark: write them as the very next thing after the mark, anywhere on the page ("right after" could be read as "to the right of").
- The plugin is now called **Manuscript** (it was Textbox Tools); the lasso panel for split, join and clean-up keeps the name Textbox Tools. It installs over the old version, with your settings and pen edits. **Move fonts you copied to `MyStyle/TextboxHelper/fonts` to `MyStyle/Manuscript/fonts`**: the old folder is no longer read. Files the plugin saves to EXPORT start with `manuscript-`.
- Tagged text without a mark keeps a "?" on the page instead of a number; numbers are given only when the text is linked to its mark and count only linked texts, so they stay in order. Select mark turns the "?" into the next number. The card after tagging says "No mark found for this text" and offers Select mark now or Later.
- Select mark on strokes that are no clear mark changes nothing and shows "Not a clear mark" with a picture of the strokes, why, Try again and Cancel.
- A number on the page always shows up in the review: a tagged mark that can't be placed (erased, over two lines, on a font plugins can't read, its caret gone) is listed with why and the nearest place it could go, instead of "not recognized". A tagged mark is no longer also listed as a stray mark.
- What the review can't use is listed only when it lies on or over the text (or is a paragraph mark in the margin): handwriting beside the text is no longer listed, a scribble over a line is one item instead of one per stroke, and a mark with its loop is one item.
- The right side shows the selected change's paragraph as it will read after all its changes ("¶ after its 3 changes"), the whole paragraph with ‹ › to page through it, opened where the selected change is: its new text underlined in bold, or, where text only goes, the word or space where it was underlined. Removed text is left out, new text is bold, text still waiting for its recognition is grey. The list on the left shows each change in its context.
- Apply applies the page shown ("Apply 4 edits on page 2": the chosen page, or the selected change's page); "All pages · 9 edits" next to it applies every page read, as before.
- The review has three tabs, New text, Marks and To decide, instead of one list of changes and the list of ignored marks. New text lists insertions and replacements with tagged text by the number written on the page, circled as there (② in the card is ② on the page); Marks lists the other changes in reading order, each with a plain symbol for its kind (− delete, / letter, ⇄ swap, ⌒ close-up, ¶ paragraph, ∧ space, , punctuation). The card opens on New text when there is any. To decide lists each item with its number (or "?", or an empty box for a stray mark), what it is and where. On the right: for a mark whose place stayed open, its picture, why, ‹ › from the nearest place, the result, Here and Drop; for text with a "?", its handwriting and text (correct it there), a small map of where it is on the page, Select mark, Insert right here and Drop; for strokes that read as no clear mark, their picture, the words under them, why, one button per mark they could be (then its place, result and Here), Ignore (remembered for the page) and Remove strokes.
- Every build says which version it is: test builds show as e.g. 0.3.0-alpha in the Supernote's plugin list, and the package is called `manuscript-<version>.snplg` (it was `textboxHelper.snplg`).
- The plugin cleans up after itself: pen edits applied or dropped more than 3 days ago are removed with their handwriting and picture, only the 3 newest backups of the last 14 days are kept, unlinking a file removes the copies of its text, and exports no longer leave a temporary file behind. Manuscript → Settings → Diagnostics → Storage shows how much the plugin keeps and has Clean up now.
- The NOTE toolbar buttons "Import Text File" and "Export DOCX" are gone: both are in Manuscript → Files.
- Manuscript: Details is now Settings, with tabs Outline · Layout · Export · Calibration · Diagnostics. Layout holds the page margins, the paragraph and heading sizes, Bold headings and which heading levels start a new page; they are used by every import (the Import screen's Layout sub-screen is gone, it shows a summary). Export holds the classification rules of Export DOCX (the Rules sub-screen is gone).
- Every linked file starts on a new page, so moving a file changes only its own pages and those it passes. Headings can start a new page too: Settings → Layout → "New page before" # / ## / ### (off by default). The import and the Outline's Apply follow both; the Outline now lays pages out within the margins from Settings → Layout instead of guessing them from the textboxes. Pages a change doesn't touch keep their layout.
- New default margins leave room for handwriting and the keyboard: about a fifth of the page on the right (280 px) and a third at the bottom (625 px). Margins set before are not taken over.
- Manuscript Outline: headings no longer move (a revision on the Supernote changes headings in place; larger reordering is done in Obsidian); the ← targets on every row, the six filter chips and the H/¶ list are replaced by the three panes. "Read again" moved to Details.
- Headings keep their #s on the page: Import writes `## Methods` into the textbox, and the #s decide a heading's level everywhere — in the Outline, in the linked file and in the DOCX export (# → Heading 1, ## → Heading 2, ### → Heading 3, written without the #s). Edit the #s with the pen and the level changes; strike them all and the heading becomes a paragraph, in the file too. Notes linked before keep working: their headings without #s keep their level.
- Import: the text file can come from any folder on the device, not only INBOX; Choose › opens in the folder of the last file.
- The lasso toolbar button "Apply Edit Markers" is gone: Manuscript reads the whole page. "Skip for later" is gone too: tagged text still being recognized stays on the page for a later pass, and Drop takes away what isn't wanted.
- Swap mark: draw a line above one part and below the next, joined by a vertical between them (|‾‾|__| or the other way round), and the two parts change places. Over several lines, three pieces are enough: a start corner, the middle piece where the parts meet, and an end corner. Punctuation stays where it was; "Fix capitals" moves a sentence's capital to the new first word. A delete or replacement inside a part is made first; a change reaching over an edge of the parts leaves the swap under To decide for a second pass.
- Insert-space mark: a ∧ or V with a small cross (+, × or #) at its tip puts a space in there, e.g. inside "thisword".
- A caret inside a word now lands on the letter it points at: the plugin measures where every letter is drawn instead of assuming all letters are equally wide.
- A step mark on its own now needs a second short line next to its upright (_||‾) to break a paragraph; a step alone is the middle piece of a swap, or is listed under To decide with how to make either. Before tagged text, a step still works alone. Steps may also be drawn falling (‾|_).
- Marks on a textbox whose font plugins can't read are now ignored instead of guessed: its words would be measured in a standard font and a mark could hit the wrong word. The review lists them under To decide with the font's name and says in its status line which font to copy to MyStyle/Manuscript/fonts. Insertions between two paragraphs still work.
- The plugin is now called Textbox Tools everywhere (plugin list, screens, permission request). Its identity is unchanged: updates install over the previous version, and the folders in MyStyle keep their names.
- Textbox Tools (the lasso screen) is new and never scrolls: the actions on the left, After and Before of the chosen action on the right, paged. Split Paragraphs is now on this screen too. An action that doesn't fit the selection is greyed with the reason ("needs exactly 1 textbox", "nothing to clean"); when only one action fits, it is already chosen. The button names the action ("Apply Join Text Boxes"); after it the screen shows the result and the backup's file name. "Log All Textboxes" moved to Details.
- Import Text File has a new screen: "Choose ›" opens the Supernote file selector in INBOX (only `.txt` files, folders inside INBOX work too) instead of a typed file name. The preview updates by itself after every change and draws the planned pages to scale; font size and margins moved to "Layout". The help is behind "?".
- Export DOCX scans by itself when it opens and after every change, and lists the textboxes that will be exported instead of the count tiles. The classification rules moved to "Rules", with Any / Yes / No for bold, italic and border.
- The plugin remembers the import settings, the export pages and rules, and the edit-marker calibration between sessions (`settings.json` in the plugin folder).
- Edit Markers has a new, calmer review screen that never scrolls. The changes are listed on the left (with a "To decide" tab for marks that couldn't be used); the right side shows one changed textbox at a time with the removed words struck through and replacements in bold. Tap a change to see it in its textbox. After Apply the screen stays open with the result and the backup's file name. Calibration and Save Diagnostics moved to "Details"; the marker help is behind "?". A font warning shows in the status line when word positions were measured in a standard font.

### Fixed

- A permission dialog closed without a choice no longer counts as allowed: Manuscript shows its permission screen instead of failing on the first file or note call.
- Manuscript Outline: the status (open, review, final) of the selected block can be set again; a long block shows its pages with ‹ › in the panel.
- Manuscript Review: a selected row's frame no longer cuts the page line at its bottom or runs into the row above.
- Imported textboxes are now written in NOTE's system font and sized for it, so their line spacing no longer changes when you edit one, and the last line is no longer cut off. Edit markers in textboxes without a font are measured in the same font, so the right words are struck.

- Import Text File looks for the file in the Supernote's INBOX folder under the folder's exact name. It used "Inbox", and opening a file there could fail with "Permission denied".

### Removed

- The lasso toolbar buttons Split Sentences, Split Paragraphs, Join Text Boxes, Clean Spaces and Remove Line Breaks, with their progress card: the same actions are in the Textbox Tools panel, with a preview first. The lasso toolbar now has Textbox Tools and Insertion or Replacement.
- Replacement text in a typed line starting with # is no longer read: tag the handwritten new words with Insertion or Replacement. A replace mark without tagged text shows under To decide with how to give it some.
- The second import folder `MyStyle/TextboxHelper/inbox`: text files are imported from INBOX only.

## [0.2.2] - 2026-11-15

### Fixed

- Edit markers find the right words again in textboxes with a font you installed yourself. Under the current firmware plugins can't read the NOTE app's font folder, so the text was measured in a standard font: word positions drifted and many markers were ignored or hit the wrong words. The plugin now reads your fonts from MyStyle/fonts. Split, Join and Import size their textboxes with the right font too.
- For the fonts that come with the Supernote (for example 851tegakizatsu), the plugin has no readable copy. Edit marker review, Split, Join, Clean Spaces, Remove Line Breaks and Import now say so in a warning instead of silently measuring with a standard font.
